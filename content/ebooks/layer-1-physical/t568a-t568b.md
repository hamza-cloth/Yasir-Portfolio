An Ethernet cable has eight copper wires twisted into four pairs. T568A and T568B are the two agreed orders for putting those wires into the eight pins of an RJ45 plug. They differ in one way only: the orange pair and the green pair swap places.

## Reading the pins

Hold the plug with the copper contacts facing you and the clip underneath. Pin 1 is on the left.

| Pin | T568A        | T568B        | 10/100 Mb/s use |
| --- | ------------ | ------------ | --------------- |
| 1   | white-green  | white-orange | transmit +      |
| 2   | green        | orange       | transmit −      |
| 3   | white-orange | white-green  | receive +       |
| 4   | blue         | blue         | unused          |
| 5   | white-blue   | white-blue   | unused          |
| 6   | orange       | green        | receive −       |
| 7   | white-brown  | white-brown  | unused          |
| 8   | brown        | brown        | unused          |

Notice pins 3 and 6. The receive pair is split around the blue pair. That is deliberate, and it is the reason you cannot simply lay the colours in pairs side by side.

Gigabit Ethernet and PoE use all four pairs, so "unused" only applies to old 10/100 links.

## Straight-through and crossover

- **Straight-through:** the same standard at both ends (B to B, or A to A). This is the cable for almost everything: PC to switch, switch to router, patch panel to switch.
- **Crossover:** A at one end and B at the other. It was needed to join two similar devices, such as switch to switch. Modern ports use **Auto MDI-X** and sort this out themselves, so crossover cables are now rare.

> [!TIP]
> Pick one standard for the whole site and write it down. Most installers use T568B. Mixing standards in one building is how a "straight" cable turns into a crossover by accident.

## When it goes wrong

- **Link up at 100 Mb/s instead of 1 Gb/s:** one of the blue or brown pairs is broken or badly seated. The link falls back to the two pairs that work.
- **No link at all:** usually pin 1, 2, 3 or 6 is open or swapped.
- **Works, but with errors:** a split pair. Each pin goes to the right pin, but the two wires of a pair were separated, so the twist no longer cancels noise. Only a tester that checks pairs, not just continuity, will catch it.
